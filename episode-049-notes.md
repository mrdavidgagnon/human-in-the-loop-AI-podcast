## Sources

1. City Square Associates, in collaboration with Station Resource Group and American Public Media Group. "Community Signals: A Community-Centric Approach to Public Media's Future." 2nd edition. Cambridge, MA: City Square Associates, October 16, 2026. Publisher page: https://citysquareassociates.com/signals/ — Zotero: https://www.zotero.org/groups/2412036/items/IZI43TZ4
     - Source type: **unpublished working document** — a consultancy white
     paper. No journal, no academic peer review, no DOI. A second edition,
     revised after a first edition was presented for discussion at Station
     Resource Group's annual retreat in Hastings, Minnesota, in August 2026.
     The document names no individual authors on its cover; the corporate
     author is City Square Associates. It names collaborators inside: partners
     at American Public Media Group led by Jean Taylor and Jeff Freeland
     Nelson, and at Station Resource Group led by Bill Davis.
     - Evidence: Supports that a structured qualitative synthesis across three
     of the firm's own prior survey studies yields eight recurring themes
     ("Signals"), organized into a Service Model (Connection, Support,
     Enrichment, Joy) and an Operational Model (Trust, Engagement, Awareness,
     Infrastructure). The three underlying studies are **RUN** (Researching
     Unmet Needs; fieldwork July–November 2024; reported total sample
     n = 31,103, with the document's own footnote describing a core national
     survey of n = 10,002 US adults 18+), **Rural** (fieldwork March–April
     2026, in partnership with the National Federation of Community
     Broadcasters, funded by the MacArthur Foundation; n = 5,810, comprising
     n = 4,375 rural and n = 1,435 non-rural respondents), and **FUND**
     (Finding and Understanding New Donors; fieldwork March 2026, with Greater
     Public, CDP, and 58 local public media organizations; n = 65,412 responses
     from people who donated to a participating station in 2025). Supports
     that public media's local-level awareness deficit is severe — the
     best-quantified claims in the document: roughly a quarter of Americans
     offer an informed account when asked to describe public radio in their own
     words; seven in ten cannot recall a single public radio station; fewer
     than one in ten correctly name a local one; nearly half of rural Americans
     are unfamiliar with the concept of community radio. Supports that interest
     in practical services (emergency alerts, event calendars, health and
     safety announcements) does **not** depend on prior familiarity with public
     media, while interest in institutional-identity content (local culture,
     history, artists, musicians) does. Supports that nearly half of Americans
     say polarization has led them to pull back from news altogether, and that
     nearly a quarter of donors sense a liberal slant in public media, far
     outnumbering those sensing a conservative one.
     - Boundaries: **Almost no quantitative result in this document is reported
     as a number.** Claims are stated as verbal quantifiers ("a meaningful
     proportion," "a substantial portion," "relatively few") with a
     parenthetical study-and-question notation (e.g. Rural Q30) identifying
     which survey item the observation rests on. No effect sizes, confidence
     intervals, denominators, base rates, or toplines are reported anywhere.
     The document offers the original **questionnaires** for download at its
     publisher page, not the data or the toplines — so a reader can verify what
     was asked, and cannot verify what anyone answered. Every quantitative
     claim is therefore unverifiable at the number level, and the episode
     reports the document's verbal quantifiers as verbal quantifiers rather
     than converting them.
     - Boundaries, continued: "Meta-analysis" is **not** used in the
     statistical sense — there is no pooled effect estimate, no weighting, and
     no heterogeneity analysis; it is a thematic synthesis. The document's own
     stated biggest limitation is that the three studies **were not designed to
     be combined and analysed together**: questionnaires and samples were built
     for separate objectives, related questions were not necessarily asked in
     similar ways, many unmet-needs questions from RUN and Rural were never
     asked of the FUND donor sample, and many public-media-specific questions
     from FUND were not positioned for a general-public audience. The document
     also states that FUND did not, by design, address the needs and behaviours
     of non-donors — so roughly two-thirds of the aggregate "100,000+ US
     adults" headline is a sample composed entirely of recent donors, and a
     claim carrying a FUND question number is a donor finding, not a
     general-public one. Claims carrying a Rural question number rest on a
     sample deliberately weighted toward rural America. The RUN headline
     n = 31,103 is **not fully reconciled** in the document against its
     described core survey of n = 10,002; the postscript accounts for part of
     the gap by noting a control sample of local station members and
     constituents (n = 8,517) that the meta-analysis deliberately excluded, and
     the remainder is not itemised.
     - Boundaries, continued: **The Community Signals Network diagram measures
     the questionnaire, not the population.** The document's own caption states
     that each node's number is the sum of its shared questions across all its
     pairings, counted once per Signal it shares them with, and that line width
     reflects the number of questions shared by that pairing. Two Signals are
     therefore "connected" when the same survey item was used as evidence for
     both — an artefact of the instrument plus the question-to-theme coding, not
     an empirical association among respondents. The document's caption does
     note that lower numbers indicate Signals measured more uniquely rather
     than Signals of lesser importance, and that low-overlap pairings are not
     necessarily weaker. It does not state the stronger consequence: the
     diagram is not independent evidence for the gestalt/integration claim it
     is deployed to support, and the conclusion's verb — that the integrated
     model **demonstrates** that service and operations are interdependent —
     outruns the evidence presented, which contains no outcome measure of
     operational sustainability at all.
     - Boundaries, continued: the directional claims that make the framework
     strategically attractive are cross-sectional associations or retrospective
     self-reports, not causal findings. That trust rises and falls with
     institutional presence rests on two self-reports (community distress and
     institutional trust) collected from the same respondents in the same
     instrument, where general dissatisfaction depressing every rating at once
     would produce the same pattern. That awareness produces goodwill is
     equally consistent with selection — and the document itself reports that
     the least aware skew somewhat more conservative, are notably more racially
     and ethnically diverse, and are less civically engaged, less culturally
     active and less driven by a wide roster of causes, i.e. they differ
     systematically on dimensions predicting baseline sympathy for the
     institution. That giving deepens engagement rests on donors'
     post-hoc self-reported behaviour change, a measure whose error runs in a
     predictable direction. The engagement-exceeds-awareness paradox compares a
     generous did-you-engage self-report against unaided recall of a station
     name, a famously harsh measure; no aided-recognition measure is reported.
     The stated preference for trustworthiness over affirmation is a stated
     preference; no behavioural measure is reported.
     - Boundaries, continued: the document's "peer review" is an internal
     review of framework and evidence strength conducted as its third analytic
     step, not external academic refereeing; it does not report who the
     reviewers were, what criteria they applied, or what the review changed.
     The face-validity claim rests on 40+ in-depth interviews with public media
     thought leaders conducted in an earlier project phase without view of the
     meta-analysis findings — convergence among practitioners about their own
     field, not independent corroboration from the communities being described.
     The document states that its subgroup exploration (demographic groups from
     RUN, donor segments from FUND, rural/non-rural) served to **fortify**
     themes already developed from the topline samples, which is a confirmatory
     rather than exploratory use of the segments, in a framework whose most
     actionable findings are explicitly about segments.
     - Genuine strengths, stated for balance: two of the three underlying
     studies (RUN and Rural) deliberately sampled the **general public** rather
     than existing public media audiences, with minimal screening (only
     omitting minors under 18) and samples actively managed to reflect the
     demographic and geographic profile of the American public — an unusual and
     expensive choice in sector research. The document publishes its
     questionnaires, carries a dedicated "Strengths and Limitations" postscript
     that names the limitations that would most change its conclusions, and
     includes a direct criticism of its own commissioning field (that public
     media practitioners have sometimes adopted the market-based framework of
     their commercial counterparts so fully that their public service mission
     has receded into the background).
     - Disclosed by the authors: the firm states it used a large language model
     (Claude, Opus 4.8) for analytic assistance in limited and specific ways —
     to help identify and map specific questions to specific Signals, to
     quantify the degree of overlap between Signals in terms of the datapoints
     used as evidence, and to generate the images for the Signals Framework and
     the inter-Signal association diagram — and states explicitly that Claude
     was not used for interpretation or copy drafting. Boundary on that
     disclosure: the question-to-theme mapping determines what counts as
     evidence for what, and the overlap quantification produces the network
     diagram; the document reports no inter-rater reliability check, human
     audit, or prompt for that step, and does not report that none occurred.
     - Structural interests, disclosable from the document's own face: Station
     Resource Group and American Public Media Group enlisted City Square
     Associates to perform the work; the firm names those two organizations'
     leaders as collaborators who helped translate the Signals into messaging
     for public media professionals; the first edition was presented at one
     sponsor's annual retreat and revised in light of that discussion; and the
     conclusion recommends that executive leaders act on the framework
     independently and in collaboration with a list of industry bodies that
     includes both sponsors. The episode states these facts and draws no
     inference about anyone's state of mind, about which the document offers no
     evidence.
