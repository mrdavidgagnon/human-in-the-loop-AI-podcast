## Sources

1. Zhou, Yiqiu; Paquette, Luc; Liu, Qianhui; Kim, Hyeongjo; and Fan, Aysa. "Using Peak Detection to Guide Qualitative Video Analysis in an Open-Ended Learning Environment." In *Advances in Quantitative Ethnography: 8th International Conference, ICQE 2026, Hiroshima, Japan, November 9-13, 2026, Proceedings*, Communications in Computer and Information Science vol. 3227, pp. 3-17. Springer Nature Switzerland, 2027. [Open in David Gagnon's Zotero library](https://www.zotero.org/groups/2412036/items/JFVEUJEP)
   - [DOI or publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-032-44026-6_1)
     - Source type: **peer-reviewed full conference paper**, 15 pages in a
     Springer CCIS proceedings volume, with methods, results and a
     limitations section. All five authors are at the University of Illinois
     Urbana-Champaign. Bibliographic note: the conference was held November
     2026 and Springer's copyright line on the paper reads 2027, which is
     ordinary for proceedings spanning a year boundary; Zotero records the
     item year as 2027.
     - Evidence: Supports a **methodological proof of concept** — that peak
     detection applied to relabelled log data can convert a continuous
     action stream into a bounded, inspectable set of moments for
     qualitative video review. Supports the **descriptive finding** that
     block modification in this environment is internally heterogeneous:
     the same relabelled log category (Modify Self or Modify Others)
     spanned task-relevant actions (structural adjustment, design
     experimentation) and task-divergent ones (playful modification,
     disruptive removal). Supports a **preliminary, exploratory codebook**
     of seven building-action patterns (space clearing, error repair,
     material replacement, structural adjustment, design experimentation,
     playful modification, disruptive removal) and a set of **candidate log
     features** (block age at removal, replacement after removal, material
     change, amount removed, removal speed, shape of removal, rework cycle).
     Supports the reported parameterization and its sensitivity checks:
     30-second windows retained after testing 0.5-, 1- and 2-minute windows
     with stable peak timestamps; a height threshold at 40% of each
     student-action series maximum, which in this data exceeded mean + 3
     standard deviations across all camps and both focal actions (those
     values ranging from roughly 6% to 27% of the maximum); and a minimum
     peak distance of 30 windows (15 minutes).
     - Boundaries — study design: An explicitly labelled **proof of concept**
     and **feature-discovery step**, not a completed measurement model. No
     learning outcome is tested and no intervention is evaluated anywhere in
     the paper. Nothing in it establishes that any of the seven patterns is
     beneficial or harmful for learning.
     - Boundaries — scale and setting: The threshold check used all available
     log data, but the video analysis covered **22 students and 187 reviewed
     clips** (115 from Modify Self peaks, 72 from Modify Others peaks), drawn
     from the two camps with usable screen recordings. 192 peak-linked clips
     were extracted and 5 were excluded as missing, corrupted or too short.
     Participants were middle-school students at summer camps running the
     WHIMC Mars-habitat activity; to preserve anonymity the authors
     deliberately do not name the camps or report per-camp counts, so total
     enrolment and cross-site comparison are unavailable by design. Setting
     is a single Minecraft-based environment during a week-long camp whose
     final two days were the team Mars-habitat build. IRB-approved with consent and
     assent procedures appropriate to the camp context.
     - Boundaries — the science-interest correlations: Modify Others
     correlated negatively with science interest for count (r = -.38) and
     proportion (r = -.26); Modify Self likewise for count (r = -.28) and
     proportion (r = -.44). These are **selection devices, not findings**.
     The paper states that science interest is not treated as the primary
     outcome and that these associations provided an empirical basis for
     choosing which categories warranted closer qualitative review. No
     significance tests or confidence intervals are reported for them, the
     sample is small, and science interest was measured once on day one
     against building behaviour from the final two days. The episode's own
     caution that the seven-way heterogeneity makes any such correlation an
     unknown mixture of distinct activities is editorial synthesis, not a
     claim the paper advances.
     - Boundaries — coding and reliability: The codebook was developed
     **inductively**, so the authors did not compute formal inter-rater
     reliability; they argue credibility rests on independent initial review
     by four researchers with non-overlapping student assignments,
     comparison of candidate codes across clips, and documentation of the
     consensus process. The codebook has not been shown to be reproducibly
     applicable by other coders; the authors' own first limitation is that
     it needs applying to a shared clip set with systematic coding
     procedures. Codes requiring inferred intent were deliberately dropped
     (a functional-versus-decorative distinction was not retained).
     - Boundaries — candidate features: Explicitly **not validated measures**
     and they do not classify clips into codebook categories. The paper's
     "related pattern" column is reported as context for where reviewers
     found each cue useful, **not** as a validated mapping, and the authors
     decline to report pairing frequencies because the review was not a
     systematic coding pass and such counts would overstate the
     relationship. None of the seven features has been computed on this
     data.
     - Boundaries — the sampling method itself: The authors note that
     peak-based sampling **privileges concentrated activity** and may
     overlook isolated edits or consequential decisions involving little
     activity; they call for comparison against random, stratified or
     event-centred samples. They further note some distinctions may be
     unresolvable from log and video alone — play versus design
     experimentation, disruption versus negotiated peer repair — possibly
     requiring self-report or peer-interaction data.
     - Funding disclosure as printed in the paper: supported by the National
     Science Foundation under Grant No. DRL-2301172. The authors state they
     used ChatGPT for language editing and proofreading and reviewed all
     resulting changes, and declare no competing interests.

1. Geertz, Clifford. *The Interpretation of Cultures*. Basic Books, New York, 1973.
   - [Open in David Gagnon's Zotero library](https://www.zotero.org/groups/2412036/items/CCSVANTP)
   - Background claim supported: the thin/thick description distinction that
     the focal paper invokes when it says log data provide a relatively thin
     description of what students are doing. The twitch-versus-wink
     illustration used in the episode is Geertz's well-known example of the
     distinction (itself drawn from Gilbert Ryle) and is offered as
     explanation of the concept, not as a claim from the focal paper.
   - Boundary: a foundational anthropological essay collection, not empirical
     evidence about learning environments or log data.

2. Shaffer, David Williamson. *Quantitative Ethnography*. Cathcart Press, Madison, WI, 2017.
   - [Open in David Gagnon's Zotero library](https://www.zotero.org/groups/2412036/items/C8495HB2)
   - Background claim supported: the foundational statement of quantitative
     ethnography (QE) and its commitment to keeping quantitative
     representations of activity anchored to the situated meaning of that
     activity — the tradition the focal paper positions itself within and
     whose usual qualitative-first workflow it deliberately inverts. The
     episode notes Shaffer is at UW–Madison as a community connection only;
     no view is attributed to him.
   - Boundary: a methodological monograph establishing a framework, not an
     empirical study of the environment or behaviours discussed here.

3. Goldhammer, Frank; Hahnel, Carolin; Kroehne, Ulf; and Zehner, Fabian. "From Byproduct to Design Factor: On Validating the Interpretation of Process Indicators Based on Log Data." *Large-scale Assessments in Education* 9, no. 1 (2021): 1-25. DOI: https://doi.org/10.1186/s40536-021-00113-5
   - Background claim supported: the contextual dependency of log events —
     that the same recorded behaviour can mean different things depending on

     the formal statement of the interpretive problem the focal paper

   - Boundary: a validity-theory contribution about process indicators in
     assessment contexts; it frames the inferential problem rather than
     supplying evidence about Minecraft-based building activity.
