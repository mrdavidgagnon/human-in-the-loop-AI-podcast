## Episode synthesis

The formulation “keep the promise stable; keep the production adaptable; keep
the judgment answerable” is this episode's synthesis across Stewart's cases,
not a model tested by the book. The cases suggest that a continuing
public-service format can combine original production, acquisition,
coproduction, presentation, scheduling, and revision while preserving an
editorial purpose. They also suggest that durability depends on human choices
about funding, selection, framing, evidence, and institutional responsibility.
Because the source selects remembered successes, it cannot establish that these
conditions cause durability or explain projects that never gained comparable
support.

Applications to public media for learning, learning games, and research
infrastructure are present-day editorial implications rather than Stewart's
findings. A durable or popular learning experience still requires explicit
learning claims and outcome evidence. A multi-contributor media or research
system still requires provenance, contestable standards and constructs,
permission and governance, revision history, and named human accountability.
