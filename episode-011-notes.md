Oxford University Press (2014).    - Evidence: a scholarly handbook chapter organizing conceptual and empirical literature on how spatial properties, auditory background, sound quality and content, and consistency within and across modalities may contribute to system immersion, place illusion, plausibility illusion, and virtual body ownership. It reviews findings involving silence, spatialized sound, room acoustics, binaural simulation, auditory feedback, and expectation-consistent sound.
   - Boundary: this is a conceptual review chapter, not a new experiment, systematic review, or meta-analysis. The underlying studies use different systems, stimuli, comparisons, and measures, and the chapter sometimes advances theoretically plausible relationships where direct evidence was sparse. Its conclusion that auditory stimuli should be considered necessary for systems intended to elicit response-as-if-real is an integrative design argument, not a universal causal effect estimate. Presence does not itself establish learning, transfer, accessibility, or benefit.

Niels Christian Nilsson, Rolf Nordahl, and Stefania Serafin. “Immersion Revisited: A Review of Existing Definitions of Immersion and Their Relation to Different Theories of Presence.” *Human Technology*, 12(2), 108–134 (2016). [Open in the Field Day Zotero library](https://www.zotero.org/groups/2412036/field_day/items/TG6FGACL) · [DOI and publisher record](https://doi.org/10.17011/ht/urn.201611174652)
   - Evidence: a review of definitions from virtual-environment, videogame, and literary-fiction research that proposes three dimensions: immersion as a property of a system, subjective absorption in narrative, and subjective absorption in intellectual or sensorimotor challenge. The article maps those dimensions against four theories of presence and explains why measures of system capability, focused attention, and the sensation of being there cannot be treated as interchangeable.
   - Boundary: the authors explicitly state that the literature review is not exhaustive. The three-dimensional taxonomy is a conceptual synthesis intended to clarify terminology and measurement choices; it is not a universally validated ontology or evidence that any one form of immersion causes presence, learning, engagement, or transfer.

## Episode synthesis

The distinction among system affordances, narrative or challenge-based absorption, a user’s experience of presence, and evidence of learning is this episode’s synthesis. The place-illusion/plausibility-illusion framework is presented only as it is described in the two fully cited focal sources; the episode makes no independent historical attribution. Neither source validates automated inference of presence from gaze, movement, speech, play traces, or multimodal data. Such uses would need explicit construct definitions, evidence connecting signals to interpretations across people and settings, direct evaluation of intended outcomes, accessible alternatives, contestability, and human review of consequential decisions.
