# Episode 001 — When AI Joins Educational Inquiry, What Should Remain Human?

Approximately fifteen minutes; five focal articles on AI-assisted qualitative
inquiry, ethics, and teacher-facing learning analytics.

This is an AI-generated summary. It can orient listeners and connect ideas, but
it may contain errors and is not a substitute for reading the cited research.
Dr. Rowan Field is a fictional composite research correspondent. No cited
author was interviewed or speaks through Rowan; the dialogue is synthesis and
paraphrase and preserves the limits of the evidence.

## Focal sources

1. Xiner Liu, Andres Felipe Zambrano, Ryan S. Baker, Amanda Barany, Jaclyn
   Ocumpaugh, Jiayi Zhang, Maciej Pankiewicz, Nidhi Nasiar, and Zhanlan Wei,
   “Qualitative Coding with GPT-4: Where It Works Better,” *Journal of Learning
   Analytics* 12(1), 2025, 169–185.
   - [Zotero file (access required)](https://www.zotero.org/djgagnon/items/IHZ6SW8U)
   - [DOI](https://doi.org/10.18608/jla.2025.8575)
   - **Evidence note:** A comparative methods study across three educational
     datasets and several coding approaches. It supports the claim that GPT-4's
     coding performance varies by construct, context, and method. It does not
     establish a universally best prompting strategy or validate automated
     coding for every qualitative tradition.

2. Kien Nguyen-Trung, “ChatGPT in Thematic Analysis: Can AI Become a Research
   Assistant in Qualitative Research?” *Quality & Quantity* 59, 2025,
   4945–4978.
   - [Zotero file (access required)](https://www.zotero.org/djgagnon/items/5USNNVIP)
   - [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11135-025-02165-z)
   - **Evidence note:** A methodological demonstration of Guided AI Thematic
     Analysis using selected material from eleven interviews. It illustrates a
     human-led workflow and reports both useful assistance and inconsistent,
     context-limited output. It is not a broad benchmark or evidence that AI can
     independently conduct reflexive thematic analysis.

3. Rudolf Messner, Samuel Smith, and Carol Richards, “Artificial Intelligence
   and Qualitative Data Analysis: Epistemological Incongruences and the Future
   of the Human Experience,” *International Journal of Qualitative Methods* 24,
   2025.
   - [Zotero file (access required)](https://www.zotero.org/djgagnon/items/LQFTQC9S)
   - [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/16094069251371481)
   - **Evidence note:** A qualitative experiment comparing human-only and
     human–AI analysis of an existing dataset. It documents disconnected output
     and weak descriptive and theoretical categories in that experiment and
     proposes a three-step rigor framework. It is not a comprehensive test of
     all models, prompts, datasets, or qualitative paradigms.

4. Edward Dieterle, Chris Dede, and Michael Walker, “The Cyclical Ethical
   Effects of Using Artificial Intelligence in Education,” *AI & Society*,
   first published online in 2022 (volume 39, 2024, 633–643).
   - [Zotero file (access required)](https://www.zotero.org/djgagnon/items/EBH84L9P)
   - [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00146-022-01497-w)
   - **Evidence note:** A synthetic review organizing ethical effects into five
     connected divides: access, representation, algorithms, interpretation, and
     citizenship. It is a conceptual synthesis and agenda for reflection, not
     an intervention trial demonstrating that one oversight practice eliminates
     inequity.

5. Chao Yang, Deliang Wang, and Gaowei Chen, “Chat-LAD: Enhancing Teacher
   Understanding of Learning Analytics Dashboard with AI-Empowered
   Explanations,” in *Proceedings of the Twelfth ACM Conference on Learning @
   Scale*, 2025, 197–201.
   - [Zotero file (access required)](https://www.zotero.org/djgagnon/items/TX2GSC4L)
   - [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1145/3698205.3733922)
   - **Evidence note:** A short design paper and case study describing an
     AI-supported explanatory interface for learning analytics dashboards. It
     supports feasibility and design discussion; it does not establish improved
     teaching decisions or student learning outcomes.

## Background source

- Virginia Braun and Victoria Clarke, “Using Thematic Analysis in Psychology,”
  *Qualitative Research in Psychology* 3(2), 2006, 77–101.
  - [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1191/1478088706qp063oa)
  - **Why it appears:** This widely used methodological article presents a
    six-phase guide and explains thematic analysis as a flexible method for
    identifying and interpreting patterns. The episode uses it only to orient
    listeners to thematic analysis; it does not attribute all forms of thematic
    analysis to these authors or use it as evidence about AI performance.
