# Episode 007 — When Does a Game Trace Become Evidence?

Playful, dramatized interview with fictional research correspondent Dr. Rowan Field. Rowan is a fictional composite; no cited author was interviewed or is speaking through Rowan. ## Sources

1. Wenyi Lu, Joe Griffin, Troy D. Sadler, James Laffey, and Sean P. Goggins. “Serious Game Analytics by Design: Feature Generation and Selection Using Game Telemetry and Game Metrics: Toward Predictive Model Construction.” *Journal of Learning Analytics*, 10(1), 168–188 (2023). [Open in David Gagnon's Zotero library](https://www.zotero.org/djgagnon/items/EVXUJ7VA) · [DOI and open-access article](https://doi.org/10.18608/jla.2023.7681)
   - Direct quote used: “we recommend co-designing the game and logs to ensure a connection between learning goals and the gameplay” (p. 183). This is the authors’ design recommendation, not a universally validated telemetry rule.
   - Evidence boundary: first field test of Mission HydroSci, a middle-school water-science game; 373 students completed Unit 3 and roughly 60 features were analyzed. The authors report missing important external pre/post assessments and describe the work as a foundation for future validated prediction models, not causal proof or a universal learner detector.

2. Ibtissem Daoudi. “Learning Analytics for Enhancing the Usability of Serious Games in Formal Education: A Systematic Literature Review and Research Agenda.” *Education and Information Technologies*, 27, 11237–11266 (2022). [Open in David Gagnon's Zotero library](https://www.zotero.org/djgagnon/items/E4V92SVH) · [DOI and open-access article](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10639-022-11087-4) · [PubMed Central full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9061227/)
   - Direct quote used: “One limitation of this SLR was that the number of included papers was small (n = 80)” (p. 11261). The quotation marks the review’s scope limitation, not a claim that the field lacks evidence altogether.
   - Evidence boundary: systematic review of 536 screened records, with 80 papers included after staged screening. The taxonomy organizes pedagogical, contextual, game, and technical dimensions; it does not provide a pooled causal effect. Generalization is limited by the search/database choices and the predominance of digital games and formal-education settings.

3. Pieter Wouters, Erik D. van der Spek, and Herre van Oostendorp. “Measuring Learning in Serious Games: A Case Study with Structural Assessment.” *Educational Technology Research and Development*, 59(6), 741–763 (2011). [Open in David Gagnon's Zotero library](https://www.zotero.org/djgagnon/items/3T6P8QGD) · [DOI and open-access article](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11423-010-9183-0)
   - Direct quote used: “structural assessment measures an individual’s understanding of a domain at least differently from verbal assessment” (p. 757). “Differently” is not presented as “always better.”
   - Evidence boundary: case study using *Code Red: Triage*, comparing verbal assessment with Pathfinder structural assessment of concept relations. The participant count was low, instruction lasted 15 minutes, and the study lacked a valid post-training performance measure for predictive-validity comparison. Results support complementary assessment perspectives, not a universal structural score.

## Connections and ongoing themes

This episode extends Episode 002, “Can We See Learning in the Game?”, by moving from trace collection and analytic models to the design of the evidence argument itself. It follows Episode 003, “When a Game Needs a Teacher?”, by asking what teacher intervention requires from an interpretable trace; it echoes Episode 004’s distinction between experience and learning; and it carries Episodes 005–006 forward by treating design guidance and playful assessment as bounded resources rather than guarantees. The OpenGameData theme is provenance-rich, contestable data: version the game, log schema, construct, assessment model, referent, transformation, uncertainty, and human decision.
